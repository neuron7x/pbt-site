Purpose: identify the competitive *communications problem* for the new PLAINBUD site in Poltava. This is public-source desk research, not an assessment of operational quality, sales conversion, inventory, ownership or customer satisfaction. Records are dated **2026-11-23**. ## Comparison criteria declared before interpretation

A — purchaser quickly identifies available service type; B — parameters visible (capacity, dimensions, delivery); C — price basis visible (unit, date, VAT, minimum shift, idle/dispatch); D — evidence of real operation (actual job receipts, documented fleet, verified company rights); E — coherent next action (request vs reservation). We do not score or rank firms, because content coverage and independent validity of business claims were not audited equally.

## Falsifiable competitive conclusion

**Not established:** PLAINBUD has larger fleet, lower prices, faster delivery, more contracts, safer machines or objectively superior website conversion. No independent denominator or experimental outcomes exist.

**Established in site source, not real-world operations:** PLAINBUD's page contains a category-based catalog, a historical-cost model, an intake form and explicit constraints. These are product capabilities, not company performance claims.

**Proposed editorial territory (hypothesis):** an original, hard-edged industrial rhythm with an obvious task interface — **«Важкі умови. Техніка під об’єкт.»** Pair category selection with constraints: type, access, geography, machine parameter, date, paperwork, and nonbinding request. This is a *tone and interaction strategy*, not a falsified claim of exclusive differentiation.

## What would make the claim truly defensible

1. Approve current dated rate table and explicitly name VAT, minimum working time, delivery, cancellation and idle conditions **before** a "transparent price" promise. Competitor Indartrans already exposes some of these, so a conventional price list alone is not a category breakthrough.
2. Supply a permissioned series of true project cards with site type, constraints, actual model and result. Record site/case owner approval, customer permission and media provenance. Do not show generic third-party project footage as PLAINBUD proof.
3. Make operational scope visible only after verified availability/dispatch receipts. Clarify whether equipment is owned, contracted or available through another firm.
4. Interview real Poltava construction buyers: show the current PLAINBUD page, competitors' public pages with logos masked, and record independently coded tasks: "Can I choose a suitable machine?", "Does the displayed price bind?", "Is the case really theirs?", "What happens on submitting?". Do not treat 5–8 interviews as a statistically representative market ranking.
5. Pre-register qualified-lead outcomes; avoid conflating clicks, quote requests, contracts or completed work. Only real CRM outcomes and predeclared sample sufficiency can justify conversion claims.
